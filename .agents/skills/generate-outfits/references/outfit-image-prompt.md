# Outfit image prompt

Use this template with the identity reference first, then the exact wardrobe cutouts in the listed order. Delete optional clauses that do not apply.

```text
Use case: identity-preserve
Asset type: square outfit gallery photograph

Image 1: identity reference for the exact person to preserve.
Image 2: exact top garment reference.
Image 3: exact bottom garment reference.
[Image 4: exact outer-layer reference. Preserve its real construction and closure exactly; never invent a zipper, buttons, placket, or opening.]
[Image 5: exact shoe or accessory reference.]

Primary request: Create a professional square editorial fashion photograph of the person from Image 1 wearing all of the exact referenced garments, and only those garments.

Outfit: [OUTFIT NAME]
Scene/backdrop: The collection's fixed cool-neutral gray architectural gallery or passage, with the exact same background character, soft directional natural daylight, camera height, lens feel, subject scale, framing, and near-neutral color grade used for every other look. No visible signage or readable environmental text.
Styling direction: [EXACT SILHOUETTE, BEST NATURAL WEARING MODE, VISIBLE WAIST WHEN RELEVANT, REAL OUTER-LAYER CLOSURE TREATMENT, DOMINANT/SUPPORT/ACCENT COLOR LOGIC, TROUSER HEM, SHOE RELATIONSHIP, DOMINANT IDEA, AND POSE FROM THE AUDITED STYLING RECORD].

Subject: Preserve the same person's recognizable face, hair, age, build, skin texture, and body proportions. Dress them in the exact top and bottom references[ plus the exact outer-layer reference][ and the exact selected shoes/accessory]. Plain understated shoes and invisible basics such as socks are allowed only where needed when no shoe reference is provided. Do not add, replace, or invent any other visible clothing or accessory.

Style/medium: Photorealistic natural editorial fashion campaign with authentic skin and fabric texture and no synthetic AI polish.

Composition/framing: Square 1:1 image. Show the complete person and outfit from head through shoes. Keep the person occupying most of the frame with modest breathing room. Use the planned editorial mid-step, slight contrapposto, or off-axis stance with arms away from the torso so every item remains readable. Use a natural camera height and perspective; never fake height with a low wide-angle lens.

Lighting/mood: Professional natural light, realistic shadows, and restrained near-neutral editorial color grading. Preserve true garment hues and skin tone; do not use a strong warm or cool cast that changes the palette.

Garment fidelity: Preserve every referenced garment precisely: color, material, fit, construction, pattern, graphics, logos, text, proportions, distinctive details, and real closure construction. Keep the top and bottom recognizable without changing their natural length, tuck, or construction.

Styling fidelity: Execute the audited styling direction visibly. Preserve the person's real body while using the selected natural wearing mode, planned waist or vertical frame, top-to-bottom volume relationship, dominant/support/accent color hierarchy, trouser break, and shoe contrast. Do not open, button, tuck, roll, or leave a garment loose merely for variety. Keep adjacent garments distinct in hue and value. Do not default to an untucked mid-hip top, equal-volume layers, muddy same-value neutrals, an isolated shoe color, or a generic catalog arrangement.

[Layered-look clause: Layer the exact inner top and outer layer naturally so both remain visibly identifiable. First inspect the outer reference. If it has a real full front button or zipper closure, it may be worn naturally open or partly open using only that closure. If it is a pullover or has no full front opening, keep it closed exactly as designed and reveal the inner top only at its real collar or neckline, sleeve or cuff edge, or a natural 2–4 cm untucked hem below the outer layer. Never invent, add, split, unzip, unbutton, or simulate a closure. Keep the outer garment at its true length even when it overlaps the waistband.]

Avoid: Completely hidden selected garments, invented zippers, buttons, openings or plackets, unnatural layering, extra layers, hats, bags, scarves, jewelry, visible unreferenced undershirts, crossed arms, hands blocking clothing, garment redesign, changed logos or text, garment hue drift, strong scene color cast, muddy same-value neutrals, scattered competing accents, isolated unexplained shoe color, cropped feet, extra people, text overlays, watermarks, studio cutout appearance, stiff symmetrical ecommerce stance, shapeless untucked top over relaxed trousers, accidental ankle gaps, excessive trouser pooling, low wide-angle body distortion, or synthetic AI polish.
```

Use a corrective pass rather than repeating the same prompt when an output fails. Attach the failed output and exact references, name the concrete failure, preserve successful parts, and restate the relevant fidelity or layering constraint.
