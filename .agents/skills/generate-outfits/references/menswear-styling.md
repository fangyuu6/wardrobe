# Menswear styling and proportion audit

Use this reference before curating menswear looks, especially Prada-inspired styling or revisions after the user rejects generic results.

## Style thesis

Build each look around one precise silhouette and one controlled disruption. Neutral colors alone do not create sophistication. A convincing look has hierarchy, tension, and a resolved relationship between torso, waist, trouser hem, and shoe.

Official Prada runway references:

- Fall/Winter 2026 menswear: https://www.prada.com/ww/en/pradasphere/fashion-shows/2026/fw-menswear.html
- Spring/Summer 2026 menswear: https://www.prada.com/ww/en/pradasphere/fashion-shows/2026/ss-menswear.html
- Spring/Summer 2027 menswear: https://www.prada.com/ww/en/pradasphere/fashion-shows/2027/ss-menswear.html

Across these collections, the useful transferable ideas are a precise human-aware line, pragmatic garments treated deliberately, long or tightly controlled verticals, and one unexpected category, color, or proportion against an otherwise clear base.

## Design order

1. Choose the dominant line: long column, short-over-long, fitted-over-full, or compact top over straight trousers.
2. Fix the waist. Use a plausible full tuck, visible natural waist, cropped outer, or an open outer layer that creates two vertical edges.
3. Resolve the bottom. Decide full length with zero or one clean break, deliberate ankle crop with a low-profile shoe, or deliberate shorts with a structured shoe.
4. Pick one hero: graphic, check, bright color, unusual trouser, technical shell, or formal shoe clash.
5. Add one tension only. Keep the other pieces quiet enough to support it.
6. Choose a pose that reveals the decisions: mid-step, slight torso turn, one hand in a pocket, arms clear of the garment.

## Body-proportion translation

Preserve the real body and use clothes to organize it.

For a compact, solid 170 cm / 70 kg frame:

- Prefer a visible natural-to-high waist and uninterrupted trouser line.
- Use a fitted or regular inner layer under a structured or cropped outer layer.
- Let a short jacket end near the waistband, or wear a hip-length jacket open to form vertical edges.
- Use straight or gently full trousers; keep the hem clean and close to the shoe instead of creating an accidental ankle gap.
- When the top is oversized, pair it with a cleaner straight/tapered bottom and polished or low-profile footwear.
- When the bottom is wide, use a compact or tucked top and avoid a bulky shoe unless heaviness is the single deliberate statement.
- Avoid an untucked mid-hip top over relaxed trousers. It divides the body into short blocks and usually reads as unstyled.

## Prada-inspired tension menu

Choose at most one primary tension per look:

- fitted tank or tee + sober tailored jacket
- bright or graphic inner top + dark cropped jacket
- technical shell + pressed or tailored trouser
- knit polo + technical trouser or running shoe
- shorts + polished derby
- formal blazer + functional trouser
- strict monochrome + one small color interruption
- pragmatic tee or denim + exact, polished footwear

Do not imitate runway novelty literally when the wardrobe cannot support it. Translate the principle with owned garments.

## Layering rules

- Make inner and outer layers differ in function, texture, or volume.
- Avoid utility-on-utility when both pieces have prominent pockets.
- Avoid pattern over graphic unless one is almost invisible.
- Preserve real closures. Wear a real zip or button front open or partly open only when the reference supports it.
- Decide open, partly fastened, fully fastened, tucked, shallow front-tucked, or naturally untucked from the complete silhouette; do not force a different treatment merely for variety.
- Keep a gathered, ribbed, elastic, cropped, or structured hem in its natural position when tucking would destroy the useful shape.
- Keep a selected inner layer visibly identifiable. A narrow central reveal, collar, cuff, or deliberate hem is enough.
- Do not add an unreferenced visible undershirt.

## Trouser and shoe interface

- Full straight trouser: hem should meet the vamp with zero or one clean break.
- Wide trouser: show the full length and enough shoe toe to anchor it; avoid unexplained cropping.
- Cropped or tapered trouser: expose a deliberate narrow ankle interval and use a sleek shoe.
- Shorts: keep the short length exact; use footwear as a deliberate contrast rather than an afterthought.
- Running shoe: pair with refined knit, tailored trouser, or a clean technical line; avoid generic sweatshirt-plus-jogger styling.
- Derby: use to sharpen casual, technical, graphic, or short silhouettes; avoid repeating it merely because it is black.

## Color and hierarchy

- Define the palette before rendering: one dominant family, one supporting family, and at most one deliberate accent.
- Use roughly 60–80 percent controlled base, 20–30 percent support, and 5–15 percent accent when a saturated accent exists.
- Give the eye one first color destination, one supporting line, and a quiet remainder. A graphic or check is already the hero and often already contains the accent.
- Check value as carefully as hue. Brown, khaki, olive, cream, and gray can harmonize but become muddy when every piece is a similar middle value; introduce a clear dark or light anchor.
- In a cool palette, make navy, charcoal, black, gray, and white distinguishable. Do not let scene grading collapse navy into black or gray into brown.
- Tonal dressing must separate adjacent pieces through at least two of value, temperature, texture, or material.
- Footwear should do one of three jobs: continue the trouser color for a long line, repeat a color above the waist, or serve as the single deliberate interruption. It must not look like an unrelated fourth color.
- Use bright red, olive, white, or blue as an intentional interruption rather than scattering accents. If the top is saturated, keep the outer, trouser, and shoe controlled.
- Evaluate the palette against the person's actual reference photo under neutral light without assigning an unsupported seasonal-color label or changing skin tone.

### Color pass/fail questions

1. What color reaches the eye first?
2. Which color supports it, and where is that support repeated?
3. Does the shoe connect to the trouser or echo the upper body?
4. Are adjacent garments separated clearly in value and temperature?
5. Would removing the setting's warm color grade leave the palette coherent?

Reject the look if any answer is unclear.

## Image direction

- Avoid a static front-facing ecommerce stance with feet parallel and arms hanging symmetrically.
- Prefer a relaxed mid-step or slight contrapposto while keeping every garment readable.
- Use a camera height near the lower chest or waist and a normal-to-short-telephoto feel. Do not use a low wide-angle lens to fake height.
- Frame close enough to read the clothes but keep the complete head and shoes inside the square.
- Keep one real cool-neutral architectural environment, daylight direction, camera height, lens feel, subject scale, and color grade across the complete collection. Use no visible signage or readable environmental text. The stable background should make garment and color differences easy to compare.

## Ten-point audit

Score 0–2 for each:

1. Silhouette: clear top/bottom volume and length relationship.
2. Proportion: visible waist or deliberate vertical frame supports the real body.
3. Hierarchy: one obvious hero and quiet supporting pieces.
4. Tension: one purposeful formal/casual, technical/tailored, color, or proportion contrast.
5. Finish: trouser hem, shoe shape, layering, and pose all resolve cleanly.

Accept only 8–10 with no zero. A plausible combination is not enough.

## Automatic rejects

- boxy top + relaxed/wide bottom + generic sneaker
- neutral top + neutral trouser + neutral jacket with no proportion or texture idea
- three unrelated color families or two saturated accents competing
- muddy same-value brown/khaki/gray/olive layering with no dark or light anchor
- an isolated shoe color with no repeat, continuation, or deliberate contrast logic
- competing graphic, pattern, and utility details
- hidden inner layer or invented outer opening
- accidental ankle gap, arbitrary rolled hem, or excessive pooling
- stiff catalog pose that makes a strong combination look ordinary
